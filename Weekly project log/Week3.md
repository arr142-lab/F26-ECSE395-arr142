### Stakeholder Interview and Problem Understanding

This week we completed our stakeholder interview and moved from the initial information-gathering stage into actually defining the problem.

From the interview, we learned more about the home environment and the specific safety concerns around the baby and the stairs. The baby is nine months old and is expected to begin crawling soon. She spends most of her time on the second floor of a four-story rental home, where there is a staircase leading down.

One of the main issues is that the parents cannot see the stairway from every part of the floor. For example, they may be in the kitchen nearby but still not have a direct view of the stairs. The family plans to use a pressure-mounted baby gate, but because they are renting, they cannot drill or bolt a gate into the walls. This means the gate provides a physical barrier but still has limitations.

We also learned more about the baby's behavior. She is curious, likes interacting with objects around her, chews on wires and cables, and pays attention to sounds and rhythms. Loud alarms can bother her, while quieter beeps or chimes are more acceptable. These details became important when we started organizing the stakeholder information and identifying the actual user needs.

### Project Background and Needs Statement

I worked on translating the stakeholder interview into the Project Background and Needs Statement assignment.

### Affinity Clustering

I contributed to the affinity clustering process using the information collected during the stakeholder interview.

We separated the interview information into individual observations and then organized those observations into themes. Our final affinity board included four major clusters:

- Parents and House Setup
- Ariana's Growing Mobility
- Alerting Without Startling
- Renter and Baby Constraints

I helped review the organization of the clusters and make sure that the information was based on actual interview observations instead of assumptions or proposed solutions.

This process helped make several patterns much clearer, especially the limitations created by the rental home, the baby's increasing mobility, the need for an alert that is noticeable without being disruptive, and the need for anything around the baby to be safe and difficult for her to interfere with.

### Personas

I also worked on the personas that came out of the affinity clustering.

We developed two different personas representing the two major users involved in the problem.

The first persona was Luis, representing the parent/caregiver. His main goals are keeping the baby away from the top of the stairs, knowing when she is approaching the stairs even when she is out of sight, and reducing the need to constantly watch the stairway.

The second persona was Ariana, representing the baby. Her behavior is very different from the parent's because her goal is naturally to explore the environment rather than recognize the danger. Important characteristics included her increasing mobility, attraction to objects and sounds, tendency to chew on wires and cables, and sensitivity to loud noises.

Creating both personas helped show that the final design will have to account for the needs of the caregiver while also considering how the baby could interact with the system.

### Key Insights

I helped review the affinity clusters and identify the major insights that came out of the stakeholder information.

The four main insights we identified were:

- The parents need to be alerted without startling or disturbing the baby.
- The problem applies beyond one household because many parents with young children also live in rental homes.
- Anything used around the baby needs to be baby-proofed.
- The level of safety risk will increase as the baby's mobility increases from rolling to crawling and eventually walking.

These insights helped us move from a collection of interview facts toward a clearer understanding of what will eventually need to drive the design requirements.
